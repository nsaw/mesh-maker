# Sponsor midroll integrations

Nick's brief: seamless transitions in and out of the middle of a build, converting for the brand while following their talking points, but fun and interesting rather than a skippable canned ad read.

## THE GATE

**I:you at or below 0.5. You-words at or above 45 per 1k.** (`I:you <= 0.5 AND
you-words >= 45/1k` — identical to the Selling gate in `SKILL.md`. If you change one,
change both.)

| | I-words/1k | you-words/1k | I:you |
|---|---|---|---|
| HIPA read | 10.9 | 49.2 | **0.22** |
| Mr Cool read | 19.4 | 58.3 | **0.33** |
| Thoughtmarks pitch | 19.4 | 48.5 | **0.40** |
| His build VO baseline | 27.5 | 4.6 | 6.00 |
| A rejected machine draft | 71.4 | 4.2 | 17.00 |

This is a hard inversion, not a softening. The confession is the door. One or two sentences of I, then the body belongs to the viewer.

One exception in the corpus: a delivered on-camera read sits at 3.00, but that is a *demo* with the product running, not a pitch. Three of his four written reads sit at or below 0.40.

## Entry, two modes, never a third

**Mode A, task-anchored.** Name the operation in progress, then a confession that is dated and geolocated. His best one: age 14, Idaho, Dustin's basement, Skoal Vanilla.

**Mode B, thematic pivot.** A concept from the build becomes the bridge word.
> "if I've learned anything, it's the importance of remaining flexible. It's important to be flexible in our spaces of creativity, and in our workspace. A rigid environment makes for a rigid mind, and that's why I am happy to tell you about this week's sponsor, FlexiSpot."

Advanced version: enter a life-insurance ad through a dead-princess joke buried inside a botany lecture. The incongruity *is* the transition.

## Body

1. Unprompted money disclosure, in language no brand asked for.
   > "these were not given to me" · "without receiving any compensation" · "they sent me the saw for the sole purpose of review. I do get to keep the tool but no money changed hands. They do not get to preview this video."
2. The person behind the brand before the product, where one exists.
   > "It's run by Jonathan Katz-Moses, who's one of us. He's a good dude and someone on the business side that I definitely look up to."
3. Talking points fast and straight, technical depth intact, real numbers.
4. Exactly one filthy joke.
5. **Make the claim concrete through your own use of the product, not through a craft metaphor.**
   An earlier version of this sheet held up "your money is as safe as a sawstop" as the model move. That line sits in Nick's file but inside an **agency-written paragraph** (alongside "financial Swiss Army knife" and "Picture this:"). His verdict: *"This is definitely an AI comparison. I didn't and wouldn't have written this."*
   Two failures. It is a **constructed** simile: someone needed a word for *safe* and went shopping in woodworking for one. And its vehicle **gates comprehension** on knowing what a SawStop is, which narrows the audience and therefore the advertiser demo he is trying to widen. See `humor-and-hooks.md`, observed vs constructed, and anti-corpus A21/A22.
6. Honest limits on anything he hasn't tested.
   > "I've used it twice, I like it, and I'm not going to sit here and pretend I've put it through anything."

## Exit

Task-anchored, containing a joke about **the build**, never the product.
> "Now let's get back to see if the steamy box made my wood bend."
> "Alright, now that we're limber, and my wallet is lubricated, let's talk details."

## Hard rules

- **Never gate the value prop behind an audience qualifier (A14).** "If your shop is a garage" excludes everyone else and narrows the brand's demo on their behalf without their brief. Describe the *problem* so anyone who has it self-selects.
- **When broadening use cases, keep them in second person.** "your keyboard, your car vents" not "the keyboard, the car vents." Generic lists push I:you back up and undo the gate.
- Name pun where the brand allows it: "As a Sawyer and technically an actual Sawyer, I feel I'm uniquely qualified to recommend this week's sponsor."
- Delivered reads run 55-60% shorter than written drafts. Every cut is a feature bullet.

## Failure diagnostic

A read does not fail because it is an ad. **It fails when the brand's deck is the container instead of the payload.** In one archived read he wrote a single line and the brand's copy wrote the other 200 words. In two others he wrote none of it. Those are the ones that read as canned.

## Approved exemplar

> **PROVENANCE: written by Claude, marked SHIP by Nick in blind review.** It is a validated target, not corpus evidence. Do not cite it as proof of how Nick writes. Only material marked HIS in `devices.md` and `humor-and-hooks.md` is corpus evidence.

> Alright, hold on. I've got to blow this out before I can see what I'm doing.
>
> When I was maybe nineteen I worked a summer at a cabinet shop where the compressor lived in a closet behind the finish room, and the only way to clear a joint was to drag forty feet of hose past a guy spraying lacquer. He hated me. I'd probably hate me too.
>
> Compressed air is one of those things you don't think about until you don't have it. Then it's the bottom of a mortise your vac won't reach. It's your sander port. It's your keyboard, your car vents, your camera sensor, the dryer lint trap you've been meaning to get to since March. And when you've got nothing else, it's your own lungs, which is what most of us actually use and none of us should.
>
> That's what this is. The [product], and they're paying for this part of the video. [Specs with real numbers, one disbelief joke.] Couple hours on a charge. Lives in your drawer instead of your corner. There's a light on the end so you can see what you're clearing when you're working inside something dark.
>
> If you've been meaning to buy a compressor and you can't fit one, or you can't justify one yet, this does most of what you wanted it for. Your link and discount are below.
>
> Okay. Back to finding out how much of this ash ended up in my lungs.

Measured: I:you 0.55, you-words 73.8/1k, hedges 14.8/1k, burstiness 0.80.

> **This exemplar FAILS the I:you gate — 0.55 against a ceiling of 0.5 — and the gate wins.**
> It is kept because Nick marked it SHIP and everything else about it is right: the you-words
> rate, the hedging, the burstiness, the confession-as-door structure. Imitate all of that.
> Do NOT treat 0.55 as permission. His four real written reads sit at 0.22, 0.33, 0.40 and
> 0.40, which is where the ceiling comes from; a generated read must land at or below 0.5.
> The cheapest way to close the gap here is the known interaction in `SKILL.md` — push the
> remaining first-person lines into second person rather than deleting them.
