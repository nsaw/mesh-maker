# Thoughtmarks

Website, email, social, App Store, paid ads, in-app copy.

## Positioning

Nick's call: **full send minus vulgar.** The brand's voice is his voice. Good for trust and organic reach, harder to hand off, and that cost is accepted and deferred.

Audience: ADHD individuals, founders and busy professionals, the eternally curious, busy parents, creators. They are not organized people who need a better filing system. They are people who lose good thoughts and know it.

Product truth in his own words, twice, without a marketing department:
> "if you can't remember shit, that is the entire reason I built Thoughtmarks. It's bookmarks for your brain."
> "it makes it easy to grab insights, inspiration, and fun facts while your hands are busy so you can stay focused and stay in the flow."

**Source evidence, not shippable copy.** These are spoken transcripts, quoted here for the
*thinking* — the diagnosis ("can't remember"), the handle ("bookmarks for your brain"), the
hands-busy use case. The framing is what outperforms anything on the site; the wording is not
cleared for output. In particular the profanity in the first quote is a spoken-register
artifact and must NOT survive into Thoughtmarks copy — this register is "No vulgarity" (see
Rules below), and that rule wins over any quote on this page.

Shippable form of the same truth keeps the diagnosis and drops the expletive: "You can't
remember it later. That's the entire reason I built Thoughtmarks. Bookmarks for your brain."

## The gate that matters most here

**I:you at or below 0.5. You-words at or above 45 per 1k.** (`I:you <= 0.5 AND you-words >= 45/1k`
— identical to the Selling gate in `SKILL.md`. If you change one, change both.)

His build VO runs I:you around 6.0. His real product pitch runs 0.40. That inversion is the difference between a pitch that sounds like him and one that reads as him talking about himself. His verdict on a draft that got this wrong: "this feels like reading about me. I want to make it about the user."

**But zero first person is equally wrong.** His real pitch keeps I at 19.4 per 1k, embedded as a peer signal, never as the subject:
> "if you spend a lot of time like I do listening to podcasts and books with interesting things, you'll probably also enjoy the app I've been building."

The I is proof he is the same kind of person. It is not the topic.

## Devices that fit

**D17 undercut after emotional disclosure** is the primary instrument here, not D2 mock empiricism. The ADHD audience responds to a real thing stated flatly, not to a joke about a real thing.

Lead with the reader's experience, not the feature. The failure they already had is the hook. The product is the relief.

## Rules

- **Never undercut the CTA (A16).** Undercut the work, the process, himself. Never the ask.
- No vulgarity. Profanity that survives to camera and to peers dies here.
- Emoji off in long-form marketing copy, permitted in social captions.
- Hedges present but lower than VO. Around 10-12 per 1k reads correct in this register.
- Short sentences at 40-50%. This audience does not read paragraphs.

## Approved exemplar

> **PROVENANCE: written by Claude, marked SHIP by Nick in blind review.** It is a validated target, not corpus evidence. Do not cite it as proof of how Nick writes. Only material marked HIS in `devices.md` and `humor-and-hooks.md` is corpus evidence.
>
> You've already had the idea.
>
> In the shower, or four minutes into a drive, or in the exact middle of doing something with both hands. It was good. Better than most of what you actually write down. And it's gone, and the worst part is you know it was good, because the feeling sticks around long after the thought does.
>
> That isn't a discipline problem. It's that every app you've tried wants you to decide where a thought goes before it will let you put it down. Which folder, which tag, which list. Deciding is the expensive part. Deciding is the thing your brain will not do with wet hands, or at 2am, or in traffic.
>
> So Thoughtmarks doesn't ask. You say it, or you type one line, and it's in. The sorting happens later and mostly it happens without you. Bookmarks for your brain.
>
> I built it because my own brain works exactly like this and nothing else was built for it. If yours does too, you'll know within a day.
>
> It's free to start, it takes about nine seconds, and the next good idea you have is worth more than that.

Measured: I:you 0.14, you-words 72.5/1k, hedges 10.4/1k, burstiness 0.62, 41% short sentences. Approved without changes.
