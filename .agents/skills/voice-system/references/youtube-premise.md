# YouTube premise packages

**Subset of the YouTube sector. Read this BEFORE `03-youtube-vo.md` and before writing a word of script.**

Nick's ranking: premise > title > thumbnail > intro > script. This sheet governs the top rung and is the highest-leverage decision available. A perfect script on a package-less build is a 50k video.

There is no single model that works. Four channels, four working models, and they contradict each other on titles, length, narration density, and tone. What they share is that each one is a **complete package**: a premise type, an opening shape, a title grammar, and a promise that the build then pays off. Mixing halves of two packages is what produces a video that tests fine and performs like nothing.

Your hierarchy, which this document assumes: **premise > title > thumbnail > intro > script.**

Below are six packages I have evidence for. Two of them are already proven on your own channel and you abandoned both.

---

## P1. Value inversion

**The claim:** this thing was worthless and now it isn't.

**Requires:** an input with a low starting valuation. A rejected slab, a damaged board, a leftover, a stolen design, a client nobody else would take.

**Open:** "Today we're taking [worthless thing] and turning it into [superlative thing]." Then an immediate self-undercut, then "let me explain," then the backstory.

**Title grammar:** superlative. Nicest, Best, Most Expensive, Prettiest. Or the inversion stated flat: "They Said This Wood Was Unusable."

**Evidence:** Salomone. 6.45M, 2.71M, 2.47M, 1.82M. This premise is the mechanism behind his 2022 break from a six-year plateau at 150-215k. The framing appears 37 times across 72,699 words of his transcripts, which means he goes shopping for it.

**Failure mode:** the inversion has to be real and legible in the thumbnail. A slab that looks fine at the start has no inversion.

**Your version:** you already buy salvage ash because it's cheap due to a dying species. You have never once framed that as an inversion.

## P2. Technical impossibility

**The claim:** this object should not work.

**Requires:** a structural anomaly in the object itself. Not a hard build, an apparently impossible one. A table that should collapse under its own weight. Drawer cabinets without drawers. Wood bent past where wood bends.

**Open:** mission stated as a goal with a difficulty claim, then the specific obstacle, then doubt about whether the whole exercise was worth it.

**Title grammar:** plain declarative, arrived at after testing. Pedulla ran eleven titles in 23 days on his 3.06M video, tried every superlative available, and landed on "A New Style of Workbench."

**Evidence:** Pedulla. 3.06M, 2.37M, 1.69M, 1.6M, 1.25M. The three-beat open is 14 of 14 narrated videos.

**Failure mode:** requires structural novelty every single time, which is expensive and does not scale. This is the least repeatable package here.

**Your version:** the hidden compartment is exactly this and you framed it as a mystery instead. "Wood does not want to be hollow" is a P2 opening sitting in your essay draft.

## P3. Jeopardy

**The claim:** something is going to go badly wrong and you should stay to see it.

**Requires:** real money, a real client conflict, or a real disaster. Cannot be manufactured without it reading as manufactured.

**Open:** what I'm making, then the promise of the future problem, then an explicit instruction to stay. Cam says "stay tuned" out loud in four of five.

**Title grammar:** a price or a quote. "$15,000 Kitchen Island." "For $35,000 I'd Expect it to be Perfect." "No table should cost $70,000."

**Evidence:** Blacktail. 18.5M, 17.0M, 15.5M. Also your own #2 video, the $30,000 Collaborative Mistake, which is the only jeopardy open in your catalog.

**Failure mode:** burns real relationships if you use client conflict, and gets stale if every video has a disaster.

**Your version:** you bury your jeopardy every time. The router that cut clean through the tabletop, the door that cupped twice, four tries at the locator pins, three at the magnet polarity. Those are all P3 promises you delivered as asides at minute thirty.

## P4. Contrarian thesis

**The claim:** everyone does X and they're wrong.

**Requires:** a category convention worth attacking, and a replacement you can actually build.

**Open:** the argument first. The build is the evidence, not the subject.

**Title grammar:** imperative or negation. ENOUGH. FORGET EVERYTHING. STOP.

**Evidence: this is yours and it is your best-performing package per minute of runtime.** ENOUGH Cutting Boards ep1 and pt2 did 367k and 493k, which is 4x and 5x your channel median, at 8 and 9 minutes. Cutting Boards pt1 hit 12.3% subscriber click-through against your usual 6.8 to 7.8%, the highest in anything I measured. STOP wasting time did 118k in 28 days with 10.6% subscriber CTR.

**Failure mode:** you need a real opinion. Manufactured contrarianism reads as bait.

**Note:** you made four of these and then mostly stopped.

## P5. Provenance and artifact

**The claim:** this material carries a story bigger than the build.

**Requires:** documentable history, scarcity, or age. A 532-year-old tree. A critically endangered species. A specific log.

**Open:** the material's story, then the object as its resting place.

**Title grammar:** the number or the fact. "from a 532-Year-Old Tree."

**Evidence: also yours. This is your single biggest video ever at 888k**, with 315,600 views in its first 28 days and 266,597 from recommendations against a typical range of 14,400 to 49,400. Also the $7,000 Bistro Table at 250k and the award-winning console at 203k.

**Failure mode:** the provenance has to be verifiable and specific. "Beautiful walnut" is not provenance. "1432" is.

**Your version, already written and unused:** "Ash is the wood of right now. Every board carries this moment inside it. It's a timestamp." That is a P5 cold open currently serving as a four-minute digression at 0:50 in a video about a hidden compartment.

## P6. Novelty object

**The claim:** I made a recognizable cultural thing out of wood.

**Requires:** pop-culture recognition. This is the "pop cultural relevance" in your own hierarchy.

**Evidence:** Salomone's giant Nintendo Switch, 8,077,268 views, the single highest view count anywhere in this dataset across four channels and 460 videos.

**Failure mode:** n=1 in my data, so treat the evidence as thin. Also does not build a furniture business, and it attracts an audience that will not convert to commissions.

## The selector

Before a build starts, in this order.

**1. Which package does this build already contain?** Not which one could you bolt on. Which one is already true.

- Is the input worthless, rejected, damaged, or cheap? → **P1**
- Does the finished object look like it shouldn't be possible? → **P2**
- Is there real money, a real client problem, or a guaranteed disaster? → **P3**
- Are you doing this because the normal way is wrong? → **P4**
- Does the material have a documentable story? → **P5**
- Is it a recognizable cultural object? → **P6**

**2. If two or more fit, pick the one you can state in a single sentence a non-woodworker will repeat.** That sentence is the title, the thumbnail brief, and the first line of the script. If you can't write it, the package doesn't actually fit.

**3. If none fit, you have a 50k video.** That is not a judgment, it's a forecast, and the honest options are: change the build so a package fits, accept it as a portfolio and commission asset rather than a growth asset, or don't film it.

Test that last claim against your own bottom ten: LEVEL UP Your Box Making, River Island Ep.2, Most Extravagant Door, Festool Track Saw Review, Midcentury Modern Bed, Multimillion Dollar Lumber Yard, Roubo Pt 4 Shop Tour, Experimental Chair, Prototyping a Planer Veneer Sled. **Not one of them fits a package.** Every video in your top ten does.

**4. Then commit to the whole package.** P2's plain title on a P1 premise will underperform. P1's superlative on a P2 premise reads as overclaiming. Cam's "stay tuned" on a P5 build has nothing to stay for. The packages are internally consistent and they are not interchangeable parts.

## What this changes about the earlier advice

The Salomone document told you to find your version of "worst into best." That's now P1, one option of six, and it may not be your best one. **Your own channel data says P4 and P5 are your proven packages**, and they're the two you stopped making. Salomone's model is worth studying for the mechanics of how he executes a package, not because P1 is the package you should run.

The cold-open findings are unchanged and apply to all six. What changes is that "open with jeopardy" was package-specific advice I gave as universal. P5 doesn't open on jeopardy, it opens on the material. P4 opens on the argument. The universal rule is narrower than I said: **whatever the package promises, the first thirty seconds states it and the next five minutes does not abandon it.**
