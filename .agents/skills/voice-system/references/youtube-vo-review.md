# YouTube VO review: retiming a delivered cut for a wide audience

**Read after `youtube-premise.md` and `03-youtube-vo.md`.** Those govern how a line sounds. `youtube-vo-assembly.md` governs writing gap VO from scratch; this sheet is its counterpart for the pass that happens once a cut exists: a DaVinci AI transcript, a worksheet, maybe a prior review, and a target audience where 95% have never woodworked. It is a checklist run before any record list is written.

**Provenance:** one build video, the iTable (Apple coffee table), September 2026. Three review passes (Caitlin's notes, a clarity pass, a layman pass) plus Nick's corrections. Everything here was observed once. Treat it as a checklist, not a law, until a second video confirms it.

## 1. The map before the words

Almost nothing that failed in the iTable cut was a sentence. It was what the viewer could picture and what they had been told the names of.

- **Draw it before you name it.** The frame was never in the plan at 1:00, then arrived at 17:44 under five different names. One exploded view with four labels fixed every later mention. If there is one graphic in the video, it is the plan.
- **Vocabulary budget: four taught nouns per video.** iTable: legs, top and shelf, frame, nubbins. Every other term gets a plain-language apposition at first use ("a bridle joint, which is...") or gets cut. Tool names (helical flush trim compression bit, PantoRouter, Domino, shoulder plane, quarter-twenty cap screws) carry zero information for the 95% and read as a club door. For a wide-audience cut this overrides the assembly sheet's "exact part numbers, tools" specificity: keep a tool name only when it is defined on contact (assembly build order, rule 4).
- **One word per part.** Subframe / skirts and stretchers / the frame and the stretchers / the skirt are one object. Pick the word, say it at first use, never drift. Word collisions on tape are real: "insert" meant steel sleeves and mosaic caps four minutes apart; "shelf" meant the table's shelf and a pocket lift.
- **No forward references.** "That would have solved this short grain dilemma" at 21:38, with short grain explained at 33:09. Replace with an open loop ("the corner problem I'm about to have") or move the definition.
- **Define where the stakes are, not where the word first appears.** The bundle-of-straws line (from the `03-youtube-vo.md` exemplar) was proposed at 1:32 as taxonomy and at 33:01 where something was about to snap off. The second placement wins. A graphic covers the first mention.

## 2. The diff, before anything else

Delivered cuts lose plants and closers first. In the iTable transcript the breadboard-guy frame, "so I can make a living doing this," the castle plant and the cutting-board closer had all been cut, and each was the setup or button for something that stayed in.

- Diff worksheet against transcript, hunting specifically for **lost plants** (anything a later callback depends on) and **lost closers** (the sentence that bookends a bit).
- Callbacks need roughly **three times the plant weight** you would assume. "Castle" in half a sentence at 40:01 could not carry two payoffs.
- Hunt for **accidental refrains**: "Wood moves" was on tape three times by accident. Said flat and identical, it becomes a chapter marker the layman learns on the first hit (D15).
- Transcript hygiene: DaVinci chops line starts; read the worksheet alongside. Check for clips parked past the end of the program (the old ad at 1:02:34) and for sponsor segments that moved.

## 3. Section triage signals

- **Self-apology brackets mark a failed section.** "Hang in there, you'll be tested on none of this" before, "Alright, wake up, your eyes glazed over" after. When both appear, cut the section, not the apologies. The post-apology is also an audience roast (banned); if a button is needed, flip it to D11: "That was a lot of words about a corner. Three of you cared. I love you."
- **Jargon concentrates in on-camera thinking-out-loud** ("the sectors won't align and it won't nest into the top and shelf"). VO replaces those. On-camera stays for what VO cannot do: "Uh-oh," dead mics and full cards, "Get it? Got it? Good."
- **Montage lead-in: stakes, not mechanism, then silence.** The last VO line before the music says what is at risk ("if the curve doesn't fit, nothing after this fits either"), never how it works. The mechanism is shown by the failure and the fix. Every attempt to explain geometry in words over a montage with no diagram died.
- **Mock-empiricism numbers need a before and an after.** "-25.2 LUFS" with no after number is just a number.

## 4. Comparisons obey the joke rules

- The **specificity floor** (`humor-and-hooks.md`) applies to explanatory comparisons, not only jokes. "800 pounds, that's a grand piano" is honest and portable. "About a hundred newborns, I've got one on the way" is honest and only works in this video, because the baby is the clock. A portable comparison is as dead as a portable joke.
- **One number per explanation.** Wood movement had three (1/8" per foot, 3/8" total, 60% RH). Keep the one a listener can picture (3/8") and replace the rest with the felt version (rainforest to desert).
- **Nearest-object entry.** The strongest route into an abstract idea is the thing in the viewer's hand: "Look at your phone. That corner. It looks round. It's not." Replaced 45 seconds of approach.

## 5. Trimming rule

When cutting for time, keep the frame and the definition and cut the numbers. The default mistake runs the other way: numbers feel like content, framing feels like fat. The prior clarity pass kept the technical defense of the breadboard decision and cut the imagined-commenter setup (D8) and the layman definition, which is backwards for the 95%. When two reviews conflict, the one that kept the frame wins.

## 6. Fact-check lane

The voice job includes the claims. Two iTable lines would have handed commenters a win:

- **Cold-open history.** "Apple commissioned a famous Danish designer" was unsupported; ScanCo made the MacTable under Apple licence and no designer is named anywhere. Check every proper noun in the first thirty seconds.
- **Named math and standards.** "Superellipse isn't technically correct but Lamé curve is" contradicts itself (same object), and a Bézier build is not "technically" either. If a line says *technically*, verify it or cut the word.
- **The graphics pipeline is a second fact-checker.** The squircle animation README flagged the same error independently. Read every supporting-graphic README before locking VO.
- **Look at the model before naming a shape.** "Each frame is a rectangle" was written from the transcript; the skirt is a ring that steps in around each leg. Any line that describes a part's shape needs a render or the OBJ open first.

## 7. Self-deprecation at scale (50M, not 500k)

- Cost rule and observed-vs-constructed from `humor-and-hooks.md` hold. A joke with a **real number** beats one with an adjective ("about five bucks an hour" beats "cheap"), and Nick's own draft with a number beat the review's line every time.
- **Compress to the last punchline.** Nick's drafts stacked two or three ("rich in personality" / "trophy husband" / "pick three"). Delivered keeps the one that makes the audience do the math themselves.
- **Spacing beats count.** Two body jokes inside ninety seconds reads as a tic. Body jokes at most three per video, five minutes apart; about ten self-roasts of any kind across 45 minutes, never two in a passage.
- **Cut at scale:** constructed wordplay that costs him nothing (the homogeneous line) and any joke whose vehicle is someone else's poverty (the Indonesian village line). Both get clipped out of context at 50M views.
- **Money disclosure is his call, never the review's.** Putting a margin on tape early (D10) earns callbacks but the client is watching. Offer it, and roll it back without argument when he says so.

## 8. The record list spec

The deliverable is read at a mic, often on a phone. What survived contact:

- **Timecode order**, one entry per change, timecodes from the current transcript (restated whenever the cut changes).
- **NOW:** the tape quote, verbatim from the transcript. **NEW:** the exact read, nothing else in the line. Then "as recorded" pointers for what stays on either side. The assembly sheet's word-count and read-seconds parenthetical is welcome on long NEW lines; nothing else is.
- **No codes.** No M-numbers, R-numbers, device tags, anti-corpus IDs or "opt" shorthand. Mark optional lines in words, in the heading.
- **No editorial suggestions inside the list.** Chapter labels, trims and graphics go in their own sections or nowhere. Nick does his own labels.
- **"Already on tape" first.** A short list of what the cut already fixed, so recorded lines are not re-recorded.
- **Graphics and deliverables as a separate section**, with their source folder and any retime (a master built to 10:59 moves to 10:35 when the cut does; say so, and fix the embedded timecode).
- When a line is revised twice in one session, the doc holds only the final; previous versions go in a one-line "rejected" note.
