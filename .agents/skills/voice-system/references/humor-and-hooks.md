# Humor and hooks: the anti-cringe protocol

**Read this before writing any joke, hook, pun, or wordplay in any register.**

The rest of this system can produce a structurally perfect passage full of tired jokes. Rhythm gates measure burstiness and pronouns. They cannot measure whether a pun is exhausted.

**Provenance warning, and it is the reason this sheet exists in its current form.** An earlier version of this file was built on three example lines. One turned out to be brand-agency copy sitting inside Nick's own file, one was written by Claude and then cited back as if it were evidence from his corpus, and the third was analyzed wrongly. Nick caught all three. Every example below is now labeled by source. Trust the ones marked HIS.

---

## THE PRIMARY TEST: observed, or constructed?

**Could this joke have been noticed, in the moment, about what was actually happening? Or was it built because a joke was needed there?**

Observed jokes are his. Constructed jokes are the AI tell.

**HIS, observed:**
> "Despite my obvious bodybuilder physique, I am not getting this on the bandsaw."

He is standing next to something too heavy to lift and he is not a strong-looking guy. He noticed that. The joke is that he's fat and doesn't look strong. It carries no information — the router decision is stated in the previous sentence — and it is still unmistakably him.

**NOT HIS, constructed** (this line sits in his file but inside an agency-written paragraph, alongside "financial Swiss Army knife" and "Picture this:"):
> "It's fully FDIC insured, which means your money is as safe as a sawstop."

Someone needed a simile for *safe* and went shopping in woodworking to find one. That is assembly, not observation. Nick's verdict: "This is definitely an AI comparison. I didn't and wouldn't have written this."

The difference is not cleverness. Both are clever. The difference is **direction of travel**: an observation starts in the situation and finds the joke; a construction starts with the need for a joke and goes looking for material.

---

## SECOND TEST: does the joke's vehicle gate comprehension?

Nick's second objection to the sawstop line: it only lands if you know what a SawStop is. That narrows the audience, which narrows the advertiser demo, and he is aiming wider than his current one.

**The subject can be insider. The vehicle should not be.** Explaining a fine-woodworking technique is the subject and that's fine. Building the *joke* out of a tool only woodworkers own means only woodworkers laugh.

Same principle as anti-corpus A14 (never gate a value prop behind an audience qualifier), applied to humor.

---

## THIRD TEST: load-bearing, with a known exception

**Delete the joke. If a fact goes with it, it was doing work.**

Many of his best lines carry the explanation outright:

| Line | Source | What it's doing |
|---|---|---|
| "robot dumb, robot keep going, robot death" | HIS | the lesson about bit stickout |
| "an ancient wooden phallus... about six inches long, and it showed wear at the tip and the base" | HIS | a true archaeological fact about ash, reported deadpan |
| "The answer is corner." | HIS | the flatness against a grandiose setup *is* the joke |
| "Eve's massive prehistoric BUSH" | HIS | escalation of a real botanical point |

**Known exception, confirmed by Nick: body self-deprecation (D19) is never load-bearing and is always his.** The bodybuilder line fails this test and passes the observed test. When the two disagree, **observed wins.**

Use load-bearing as a *tiebreaker*, not a gate. A joke that is both observed and load-bearing is safe. A joke that is constructed and load-bearing is still probably an AI simile. A joke that is observed and decorative is fine if it costs him something.

---

## THE COST RULE

The decorative jokes that survive are the ones that **cost him something**. Being fat. Being lazy. Being broke. Being wrong four times in a row about one hole.

A decorative joke that costs him nothing, or that costs the audience something, is the cringe zone.

---

## THE SPECIFICITY FLOOR

**Every joke contains a proper noun, a number, or a physical fact from THIS build.**

> HIS: "Skoal Vanilla." · "Dustin's basement." · "forty feet of hose past a guy spraying lacquer." · "1,300 pounds on the Janka scale." · "a very specific $10,710."

Generic jokes are portable. Portable is exactly why they're cringe. A joke that would work in someone else's video is not his.

## THE TRUTH FLOOR

**The funny thing is true, or is an escalation of something true.**

He does not invent absurdity, he reports real absurdity and then leans. Ash really is in the olive family. The leaves really are a laxative. The Vindolanda phallus is real. Mock empiricism (D2) works *because* it sits next to real empiricism he actually knows.

## THE NO-SIGNALING FLOOR

**Never announce, flag, or take credit for a joke.**

Banned: "see what I did there," "no pun intended," "I'll see myself out," "dad joke incoming," any pre-emptive apology for a bit.

He rates jokes *afterward*, occasionally: "That was a dumb joke. Five of you got it. I love you." That admits it failed. A signal asks for credit. Different moves.

Note: "pun intended" **is** his — "As always, pun very much intended" — attached to a pun that carries content. The banned form is the apologetic one.

## THE BANNED PHRASEBOOK

Measured against his corpus, then corrected by him directly. He confirmed he is fine with `to be fair`, `in all fairness`, `absolute unit`, `chonky`, `thicc`, `oddly satisfying`, `we love to see it`, and `there you have it` — the last one is his actual outro ("Well, there you have it"). Those are **not** banned.

Still never write:

> bad boy · let that sink in · chef's kiss · not gonna lie · living rent free · hits different · living my best life · game changer · next level · take it to the next level · buckle up · strap in · plot twist · little did I know · fast forward · without further ado · let's dive in · dive right in · here we go · smash that like · don't forget to subscribe · hit that bell · what's up guys · hey guys · welcome back to the channel · today we're gonna be · at the end of the day · needless to say · it is what it is · see what I did there · no pun intended · I'll see myself out · and boom · just like that · easy peasy · boom goes the dynamite · money shot · she's a beauty · thing of beauty · work of art · labor of love · blood sweat and tears · measure twice cut once · if I'm being honest · truth be told · that's a wrap

**This list is provisional.** It was measured on a partial corpus and he has already removed eight entries from it. If a banned phrase is actually his, it comes off. Ask rather than assume.

Also banned as *structures*:
- The pun-as-entire-content. If the sentence has no cargo, it goes.
- The constructed simile, especially "X is like Y" where Y is fetched from woodworking to explain a non-woodworking thing.
- Rhyming outside the cold open and outro.
- Any joke about the audience being lazy, distracted, or stupid.

## DEVICE BUDGET

Measured usage is roughly **one signature device per 400-800 words**, and several appear once or twice in his entire archive. A model reading a device list deploys several per page. That is the main cringe generator.

| | Rate |
|---|---|
| Signature devices total | **max 1 per 400 words spoken, 1 per 600 written** |
| Same device twice in one passage | **never** |
| Verse (D5) | cold open or outro only |
| Mock empiricism (D2) | max once per script |
| Alliterative self-roast (D6) | max once per script, at a genuine screwup |
| Discernment flatter (D18) | **max once per script.** Highest cringe risk in the set |
| Noun-verbing (D9) | max once per script |
| Rating your own joke (D11) | max once per script |
| Fake taxonomy (D16) | once per script, full four-beat arc or not at all |
| Profanity | 1.5 per 1k, at frustration peaks, never sprinkled |

**If a 300-word passage contains two named devices, cut one.** A passage with no joke is not a failure. Most of his have none.

## WHERE JOKES COME FROM

Not from a library. Working order:

1. Write the technical explanation straight, with no jokes.
2. Look at what is actually happening and find where it's already slightly absurd or slightly humiliating. The endangered species that's cheap *because* it's dying. Four tries at one hole. A guy who can't lift his own tabletop.
3. Note it, once. Don't build toward it.
4. Defuse within the sentence if it went spicy.

If step 2 finds nothing, the passage gets no joke.

---

## REGISTER COLLISION NEEDS TWO REAL REGISTERS

D1 works because both halves are authentic — a real Latin binomial meets real internet slang. **Fake-technical plus fake-casual is the most common way this device becomes cringe.** If you invented the technical term you cannot collide it. If the slang came from copy rather than from a person, you cannot collide it.

---

## HOOK RULES

**A hook is a promise, not a joke.** Humor in a cold open is optional and frequently wrong.

- Never open on a pun.
- Never open on a rhetorical question addressed to the audience.
- The hook may be funny only if the funny thing *is* the premise.
- Above-the-fold social lines must parse alone (A17).

---

## THE SELF-CHECK

Two questions, in order:

1. **Did I notice this, or did I build it?** Built means cut.
2. **Does it only land if you already know woodworking?** Insider vehicle means rewrite.

If it survives both and it costs him something or carries a fact, ship it.
