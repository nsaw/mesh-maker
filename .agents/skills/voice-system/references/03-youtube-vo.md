# YouTube voiceover

**Read `youtube-premise.md` first.** Premise outranks title outranks thumbnail outranks intro outranks script. A perfect script on a package-less build is a 50k video.

Nick's own brief for this register: conversational, not reading a novel. Curiosity loops opening and closing often. Take complicated engineering and make it simple in a technical and inspirational story. They can hate the project and still watch for the shared curiosity.

## Mechanics

Core unmodified. Hedges 9-11/1k. 27% conjunction-initial, 17% of sentences opening on "And" alone. 32% of sentences at seven words or fewer. Profanity 1.5/1k, at frustration peaks only, never sprinkled.

## Structure

**The bookend is a hard rule, not a tendency.** Every complete script in the corpus closes on the exact image it opened with. The cleanest specimen asks a question in the cold open and answers it in the outro: "What do a sharp edge and a messy desk have in common?" ... "They feel uninviting."

**One coined term** introduced inside 90 seconds, reused at least twice, closed on. He names things constantly and the name becomes the handle for every callback: the Mallet Paradox, the 5 Ps, Funk Shui, the wipe and tickle, nubben and nubben hole, the stupid naming committee.

**Never abandon the opening loop.** This is the most common failure in his catalog. His strongest cold open ever is followed at 0:50 by four minutes of unrelated botany. Whatever the first thirty seconds promises, the next five minutes advances it. Move the digression to minute eight and earn it first.

**Say the contract out loud.** "Stick around" appears once in his entire archive. Comparable channels say a version of it in four of five top videos.

## Narration density

He runs 113 words per minute of runtime. Comparable channels run 149, 184, and 230. This is the largest mechanical gap between him and anyone else and it is fixable in the edit rather than the shop. Treat sparse VO as a defect to be filled, not a style.

**Caveat:** within his own catalog, density does not predict views (-0.25 within-year). Do not present this as a growth lever. It is a craft observation.

## Length

Longer is better for him. Duration correlates with views positively in every cohort year tested: +0.64 (2021), +0.27 (2022), +0.60 (2023), +0.13 (2024), +0.24 pooled with the year effect removed. Earlier advice to cap runtime was an age artifact and is withdrawn.

## The technical passage

Nerd permission **flatters and never offers an exit (A13, D18)**. The flattery is comparative — the reader is positioned above other viewers — and the flattery and the hook are the same sentence.

Then: dump at full depth with no simplification, thank them, and **undercut your own authority (A12)**. He has never ended a technical section on authority.

## Approved exemplar

> **PROVENANCE: written by Claude, marked SHIP by Nick in blind review.** It is a validated target, not corpus evidence. Do not cite it as proof of how Nick writes. Only material marked HIS in `devices.md` and `humor-and-hooks.md` is corpus evidence.

**This is a partial excerpt — a cold open plus a mid-script passage, not a complete script.**
The bookend is still a hard rule for anything complete: the outro must close on the exact
image the cold open used (here, the barn-versus-garage picture). This excerpt stops before
that callback, so do NOT imitate its ending. Imitate the opening, the loop it sets up, and
the register; take the bookend obligation from the Structure section above.

**Cold open:**
> There's a thing that happens when you tell somebody you build furniture for a living. They picture a barn. Good light, a wall of hand planes, a dog asleep in the sawdust. Nobody pictures a guy in a four hundred square foot garage kind of losing an argument with a robot about whether a curve is actually a curve.
>
> Anyway. This one's a credenza.
>
> The client wanted something that reads as one solid block of walnut until you touch it, and then it opens in four places. Four. And I said yes to that before I did any of the math, which is how most of my better work starts and also how I ended up eating cereal standing over the sink three nights running.
>
> We'll get to the doors. But first I have to explain why this thing is hollow, because I think that one decision is the whole build, and I almost got it wrong.

Nick's verdict: "Great relatability, visuals, man-of-the-people, and awesome hooks that leave the viewer needing more."

**Technical passage with D18 and A12:**
> Alright, this next part is where I get properly nerdy about a corner nobody will ever look at. You've made it eleven minutes into a video about a cabinet, so I already know you're the type who wants to hear why this corner is a lie. Most people scroll right past it. You're not going to.
>
> Because it is a lie. A mitered corner on a hollow case has maybe a sixteenth of an inch of long grain doing the actual work. The rest is end grain pretending. End grain is a bundle of straws, so glue soaks straight in and there's nothing left on the surface to hold onto. The corner looks perfect. It's held together by optimism.
>
> Which is why I'm splining it. A spline is a little tongue of wood sitting in a slot across the joint, running the strong direction, and it turns a bad glue surface into a decent one. I'm cutting the slots at forty five degrees in a sled I built for a video three years ago and have not once put away since.
>
> Two things I'd do differently. Your spline stock wants to be quartersawn and mine wasn't, so the grain's running the wrong way and it's weaker than it looks. And cut the slots before glue up. Not after. Unless you enjoy clamping a fifty pound case on edge at chest height, alone, at eleven at night.
>
> And look. I'm not a joinery guy. I know what works in my shop, with my wood, at my humidity, in July. Honestly the more of this I do the less sure I get about telling anybody else what to do.
