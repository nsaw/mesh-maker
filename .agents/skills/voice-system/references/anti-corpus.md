# Anti-corpus

Named failure modes. A1-A7 quoted from AI drafts found in Nick's own archive. A8-A18 derived from his blind review notes. A19-A22 are provenance failures Claude made and Nick caught. Pattern-match and kill these before anything reaches him.

**A1 The AI aphorism.** Profound-shaped, meaning-free, always in the outro.
> "Because insecurities are loud; true confidence doesn't have much to prove."
> "Because the truth is: nobody else is keeping tabs but you."

Nick's philosophy always arrives attached to a physical object or a specific failure, never freestanding.

**A2 Triadic rhetorical question stack** addressed to the audience.
> "Is it the wood? Is it the craftsmanship? Or is it something intangible, like the story it tells?"

**A3 "Here's the deal" / "Here's the thing."** His actual rate: 0.11 per 1k. Effectively never.

**A4 Pre-apologized irreverence.**
> "I've been thinking—dangerous, I know—"

He rates jokes *afterward* (D11). He never flags them in advance.

**A5 Em-dash asyndeton.** Machine drafts run 3.0 per 1k against his 1.4, and his mouth splits every one of them into separate sentences.

**A6 Vibe adjectives about craft.** "truly special," "utterly necessary," "pure magic," "smooth as butter." He describes wood with species, price, defect, and behavior.

**A7 Warm open plus warm close.**
> "Hey there, folks, welcome back to the shop... Take care, everyone."

His opens are cold: mid-thought, mid-problem, or mid-aphorism. His close is fixed and terse.

**A8 De-hedged confidence.** The dominant failure. Any spoken passage under 5 hedges per 1k is a costume regardless of how good the jokes are.

**A9 Specific to generic.** Documented in his own archive, where a brand sanitized his read:
> "0/10 RECOMMEND" became "I wouldn't recommend it."
> "FELLOW WOODWORKING HERMITS" became "For fellow woodworking enthusiasts."
> "EVEN WHILE WORKING MY WOOD. THAT CAME OUT WEIRD, BUT IMMA ROLL WITH IT" became "even while working on my wood projects."

**A10 Earnest setup, joke payoff.** Backwards. See D13.

**A11 Compressed setup, inflated payoff.** Backwards. See Law 2.

**A12 Ending a technical section on authority.** He has never once done this. The undercut is mandatory:
> "it sounds like I know what I'm doing but the more I learn about finishing the more I realize I know nothing about finishing."

**A13 Offering an exit.** Skip-timestamps, "feel free to skip ahead," "the timestamp's below," "I won't know." Never hand people the door. Retention at 0:30 already runs below his own channel baseline on more than half his videos.

**A14 Gating a value prop behind an audience qualifier.** "If your shop is a garage" excludes everyone else, and on a sponsor read it also narrows the brand's demo on their behalf without their brief. **Describe the problem so anyone who has it self-selects. Never describe the person.**

**A15 Stating differentiation instead of implying it.** "Why this is not a furniture store table" is an authority claim, and he never makes authority claims. The specifics are the argument. Naming the competitor is what a furniture store would do.

**A16 Undercutting the sale.** D10 economic honesty deployed at the moment of the ask. His verdict on a draft that did it: "That's a shit line to leave a sale on." Undercut the work, the process, yourself. Never the ask.

**A17 An above-the-fold line that needs the next line to parse.** Intrigue is a complete thought that raises a question. Confusion is an incomplete thought that raises a parsing problem.
> Fails: "Eleven months, two of them arguing with a door." (Two of what? The reader hasn't committed yet.)
> Works: "The door won. Twice."

**A18 Making a sell about him.** Measured: his build VO runs I:you around 6.0, his real sponsor and product pitches run 0.22-0.40. A draft at 17.00 read as "all about me." Both a too-high and a zero first-person ratio are failures. See the selling gate.

**A19 Other creators' voices in the training data.** His scripts directory contains full transcripts from three other woodworking channels. Any tool that globs that directory ingests strangers. Verify authorship before sampling anything.

**A20 Citing generated text as corpus evidence.** Claude wrote a line, Nick approved it in review, and Claude then quoted it back as proof of a theory about how Nick writes. That is circular and it happened. **Approved exemplars are validated targets. They are not evidence.** Only material explicitly marked HIS may be used to justify a claim about his voice.

**A21 Mixed-authorship files.** More dangerous than wholly foreign ones, because you cannot exclude the file, you have to segment inside it. His NorthOne sponsor read contains agency deck copy ("financial Swiss Army knife," "Picture this:," "straight-up beautifully bundled business banking") interleaved with his own jokes ("onlyfans" planted in a list, "Just making sure you're still with me," "\*COUGH Quickbooks \*COUGH"). Extracting from the wrong half produced a canonized AI simile. **Segment before sampling: brand decks, AI drafts, and his edits coexist in single files.**

**A22 Comprehension-gating humor.** A joke whose vehicle requires insider knowledge narrows the audience and the advertiser demo. The subject may be insider. The vehicle may not be.
