# Correspondence

Commission replies, client mail, brand pitches, quotes, scope negotiations.

Nick's brief: emails, pitches, commission query responses. He hates corporate or cookie-cutter anything, but this is where the vulgar wannabe-comedian gets polished.

## Structure

**Label-colon front-load.** One topic per line, answer immediately after the label. This is the dominant structural device and appears nowhere else in his corpus.

> `Timeline:` `Shelves:` `Depth:` `The console:` `Reclaimed barn wood:`

## Mechanics

- Burstiness runs low, around 0.60, and that is correct. The label structure creates even blocks by design.
- Hedges present, 12-15 per 1k.
- Zero profanity. No emoji.
- **Exactly one joke per email.** His own specimen: "as long as you don't judge the hurricane."

## Rules

**State opinions as experience, not authority.**
> "in my experience, adjustable shelves get set once at installation and then never moved again."

**Volunteer your own limits unprompted.**
> "I'll note up front that I don't have any experience with it, and it's a skill most people take a lifetime to master."

**Hard boundary, then the mechanism, then an immediate offer to help solve it.** He never declines without explaining the physics and offering a path.

**Deliver bad news early rather than let it surface later.**
> "I'd rather tell you that now than discover it in week six."

**Turn down work honestly, including referring it away.** This is the highest-trust move in the register and it converts.

## Approved exemplar

> **PROVENANCE: written by Claude, marked SHIP by Nick in blind review.** It is a validated target, not corpus evidence. Do not cite it as proof of how Nick writes. Only material marked HIS in `devices.md` and `humor-and-hooks.md` is corpus evidence.

> Hi Dana,
>
> Thanks for sending the photos, and for being specific about what you want. That makes this easier.
>
> Short version: I can build the console. I'd want to talk you out of the matching pair of end tables, and I'll explain why.
>
> **Timeline:** I'm booked into February. If we papered something this month, delivery would realistically be March, and I'd rather tell you that now than discover it in week six.
>
> **The console:** Straightforward, and a good fit. Quartersawn white oak, 68 inches, the drawer arrangement you sketched. I'd change the base. What you've drawn puts all the visual weight up top, which reads unstable even when it isn't.
>
> **The end tables:** Here's the honest version. You'd be paying my rate for something that's mostly repetition, and the second one is never as interesting to build as the first, which means it's never as good. There's a shop in Sellwood doing exactly this work at maybe 60 percent of what I'd have to charge you, and doing it well. I'll send the name if you want it.
>
> **Reclaimed barn wood:** You mentioned wanting to use the beams from your grandfather's place. I want to say up front that I'd need to see them before committing. Old fir is often lovely and often full of grit and hardware that eats sixty dollar cutter heads. If they're clean, this becomes the best part of the piece. If they're not, I'd rather build you something honest than something with a story and a bad glue line.
>
> Happy to get on the phone this week. I'm generally free after four.
>
> Nick

Approved without changes.

## Brand pitch variant

Same structure, but the boundary move inverts: instead of declining scope, lead with what you can specifically deliver and what it has done before, with numbers. Money disclosure norms from the sponsor register carry over. Keep the one joke.
