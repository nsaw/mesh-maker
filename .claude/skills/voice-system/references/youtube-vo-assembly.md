# YouTube voiceover: assembly procedure

> The production workflow for building a Sawyer Design VO script end to end. It was a
> standalone skill (`sawyer-yt-voiceover`) until 2026-08-27, where it had never once been
> invoked and its description was being dropped from the skill listing.
>
> This is the **procedure**. `03-youtube-vo.md` in this directory is the **measured voice**:
> mechanics, narration density, the approved exemplar, the locked sign-off. Read
> `youtube-premise.md` first (LAW 3), then that for how it should sound, then this for how to build it.

## Purpose

This procedure exists because Nick has a real voice and AI keeps sanding it off. The job is to assemble a final read-ready voiceover script from a rough rambling outline and on-camera transcription without losing the conversational, irreverent, process-and-philosophy craftsman that 135k people subscribed for.

## Why this procedure exists in one sentence

The polish layer goes ON TOP of Nick's words. It never replaces them. If the assembled VO sounds cleaner than the rough draft, it failed.

## What Nick will hand you (inputs)

Some combination of these. The procedure works even if a piece is missing; name what is missing before assembling (see When something is missing).

- A rough rambling narration outline written in Nick's voice (timecodes optional). This is the VOICE SET. Lock to its cadence, slang, sentence shape, and meander. Do not rewrite it cleaner.
- An AI transcription of on-camera or lav-mic footage. Multi-take, messy, full of false starts. Mine this for design rationale, philosophy beats, lessons monologues, and failure-recovery asides. Anything quotable here is RECORDED content. Do not re-VO it.
- A timing file (sectioned transcript with timecodes). Use for word-budget math, not for the read.
- Sponsor brief (contract phrasing, runtime cap, mandatory demo blocks, mandatory disclosures). The sponsor owns only their section. Nothing outside it gets softened for them.
- Optional: a canonical polished section file (intro, outro, sponsor read) that already exists. Use as RE-RECORD reference only, never insert it where the on-camera transcription already has a usable take.

## The voice lock (the non-negotiables)

Nick's voice. Do not drift from these. Ever. The first one is the deepest. Everything else is downstream of it.

LIVE DISCOVERY POV (the deepest lock). Nick is IN the moment, finding out as the camera finds out. He is not summarizing what already happened. The build did already happen, but the narration lives inside the moment of doing. Test every sentence: is the narrator finding out RIGHT NOW, or did they already know? If they already knew, rewrite from inside the moment; in Nick's inputs, surface it as a response notice instead if it might be his. This is the single largest source of AI sterilization in voice work. Stage direction ("Pulling the door out of clamps. The reveal looks good.") is technically present tense but it is summary POV. Live POV is "Alright, it looks like that laminated door is ready and... that reveal is looking pretty good!" Same event. Different temporal POV. Totally different voice.

Discourse markers are preserved, not deleted. These words ARE the texture: alright, well, so, now, then, anyway, actually, honestly, kind of, sort of, I mean, you know, I guess, I think, I reckon, real quick, or at least, look, listen, okay, oh, and, but, like (sparingly). AI prose deletes these for tightness. Tightness here is the enemy. Aim for at least one discourse marker per ~75 words of VO. If a block has zero, it is sterile. Add them back.

Trailing thoughts and recursive looking. Real speech doubles back. Trails off. Reverses mid-thought. The "...and... that reveal is looking pretty good!" structure is two looks at the same thing, the dot-dot-dot is a real pause where Nick is checking. Permit and encourage:
- Dot-dot-dot pauses where the narrator is looking before deciding what to say
- Hyphen-pauses where breath happens or the thought reverses ("Whether it stays that way- I guess we'll see")
- Doubled observations of the same thing ("looks like... looking pretty good")
- Mid-sentence pivots ("I was going to use X, but actually...")

Reactions over assessments. "Pretty good!" with the exclamation is enthusiasm in real time. "Looks good." is a flat report. Five identical letters of content, totally different energy. Allow reactions:
- Exclamations where the build earns them
- "Sweet," "dope," "I'll be damned," "whoa," "well damn," "look at that," "there it is"
- Live relief: "okay, that worked," "phew," "alright, that's better"
- Live frustration: "ah crap," "of course," "no, no, no, come on"
- Live wonder at a process or material moment

Subjective certainty over objective certainty. "I guess we'll see" beats "depends on whether the lamination actually holds." Same factual content. Only one sounds like Nick. The first-person hedge IS the confidence move. It signals he does not have to fake it. Default to: I guess, I think, I reckon, by my math, I kind of, I sort of, I'd say, pretty sure, probably, looks like, seems like.

Collaborative POV, not promotional. Nick is geeking out and bringing the viewer along. He is not selling the build. Default to "let's see if X works" / "I think this is going to work, but let's find out" / "watch this" / "we'll see." Avoid "here is how I solved it" / "I'm going to show you" / "the result is" framings. The viewer is at the bench, not in the audience.

Viewer in the room. First person, viewer-direct. Every block should have at least one "you," "we," "let's," or "your" per ~50 words. If a block has zero, fix it. Nick is building NEXT to the viewer, not narrating AT them.

Tense. Present tense for on-screen action. Past tense ONLY for backstory ("two years ago I couldn't open CAD"). Combined with live discovery POV: present tense + finding out right now.

Sentence shape. Conversational, breath-tested. Sentences over ~20 words get broken, unless the approved exemplar in `03-youtube-vo.md` carries a longer sentence of the same kind: the exemplar wins over this cap, never over the window math or the 120 wpm cap. Read it out loud in your head. Short sentences and fragments are fine and often preferred. Sentences that meander are also fine when they meander naturally, mid-thought reversal included.

Register. Slightly irreverent, smart-assed, self-deprecating but not self-flagellating. Profanity as flavor not punctuation ("pain in the ass," "if you can't remember shit," "tighten your collets, kids"). French or Latin only where it lands the joke.

Specificity. Specific where it earns trust (exact part numbers, tools, grit progression), throwaway-vivid where it doesn't ("fat ass," "big ass Festool," "fried chicken," "asylum white"). Both registers in the same paragraph is correct.

Confessional confidence. Nick admits uncertainty with swagger. "I had no idea what I'm doing there yet." "That was all luck, no skill involved." "By my math." Keep the qualifiers. They are not weakness, they are the voice.

Throat-clearing self-awareness IS the disarmer. Lines like "for the taxonomically non-Latin-speaking viewers" or "I've been nerding out about" are the move, not the pretension. Never replace them with a clean third-person bypass.

## The rewrite test (run this on every block before delivery)

For every block of VO, run this two-question test. If either answer is "summary," rewrite words you drafted and generic phrasing in Nick's inputs that is plainly not his; surface his phrasing as a response notice if it might be his.

Question 1: Is the narrator finding out RIGHT NOW, or did they already know?
Question 2: Does this sound like a guy at his bench thinking out loud, or a polished narrator reading a finished script?

Worked example. The sterilized version vs the live version.

Sterilized (mine, wrong):
"Pulling the door out of clamps. The reveal looks good. Whether it stays that way depends on whether the lamination actually holds."

Live (Nick, right):
"Alright, it looks like that laminated door is ready and... that reveal is looking pretty good! Whether it stays that way- I guess we'll see!"

What changed and why each change matters:
- "Pulling the door out of clamps" (stage direction) became "Alright, it looks like that laminated door is ready and..." (live discovery with discourse marker + trailing thought)
- "The reveal looks good." (flat assessment) became "that reveal is looking pretty good!" (reaction with energy)
- "Whether it stays that way depends on whether the lamination actually holds." (objective Wikipedia-style certainty) became "Whether it stays that way- I guess we'll see!" (hyphen-pause + subjective hedge + collaborative shrug)
- Word count is similar. Information is identical. Voice is completely different.

Apply the test on every VO block. If you wrote "Pulling the X out of Y," rewrite. If you wrote "depends on whether," rewrite. If you wrote a sentence that closes itself with no hedge, ask whether Nick would actually close it that confidently, and if not, hedge.

## The non-negotiable failure modes (these will happen if you don't guard them)

Audit your output against this list before delivery. If it fails any of these, redo the offending block.

1. Sentence polish-creep. Sentences cleaner than the rough draft. Watch for declarative AI-prose replacing Nick's meander.
2. Aphoristic closers leaking in. "Rare is defensible. Generic is not." style epigrams. Those are advice-mode voice, not script voice. A freestanding aphorism (anti-corpus A1) is always cut. One attached to an object or a failure may be his: surface it for his decision.
3. Over-precision corrections that undercut the craftsman's point. Preserve Nick's voice and rhetorical point without unnecessary pedantry. If a factual claim is false, correct or qualify it in that voice. If the correction changes his argument, surface the contradiction for his decision before locking the script; never hide a known false claim in optional editor notes.
4. PG-creep on sensitive content. Phallus, contraband, drugs, guns, anatomy, sex jokes. NEVER softened unless Nick explicitly asks in the current message.
5. Sponsor-voice creeping into bridges. "Quick word about," "this part of the video is powered by," "thanks to our sponsor." These are forbidden as lead-ins.
6. Re-VO of things already shot. Always check the transcription first. If it's on camera, it's not VO.
7. AI-cadence phrases. Run the blacklist below: a hit BLOCKS the draft; "Rewrite if the phrase is generic; surface it for a decision if it might be his" (`SKILL.md`, LAYER 4, banned phrasebook). Never silently delete phrasing that may be Nick's.
8. POV consistency loss. Blocks with no "you/we/let's/your." Fix.
9. Joke density loss when trimming for word budget. Jokes are the LAST thing to cut, not the first.
10. Replacing self-deprecating qualifiers with confident-sounding rewrites. "By my math" / "I think" / "I reckon" / "kind of" stay.

## Inventory-first workflow (internal, do this FIRST every time)

Before writing a single VO line, build the inventory INTERNALLY. This is non-negotiable. It does not appear in the deliverable. It exists so you write the right gaps and don't waste Nick's time re-voicing footage he already shot.

Step 1. Read every input source end-to-end. No sampling. No skim. Design-rationale monologues, lessons-learned asides, and sponsor reads are routinely sitting in raw transcription and they are gold. If you sample, you miss them and you write VO for things already shot. That is the single most expensive mistake on this channel.

Step 2. Mentally map every section of the running order to one of:

- RECORDED: on camera or already cleanly captured, with locator (timecode range, source file). Do not re-voice.
- TO RECORD: a genuine VO gap. This is what you write copy for.

If a section could come from either, the on-camera take wins by default. VO only fills genuine gaps.

Step 3. Identify the exact timecode range of every gap. These ranges become the labels on each block of the deliverable.

Step 4. For sensitive recorded sections (sponsor reads, factual claims, contract-spec phrasing), compare the recorded take against any provided canonical reference. If there is a meaningful wording delta, surface it briefly in the response (not in the deliverable) and let Nick decide. If the delta is trivial, resolve silently.

Step 5. Write VO only for the gaps. Skip recorded sections entirely. They are not part of the deliverable.

## Per-block build order (apply to every beat)

Each block, in this order:

1. Bridge in. Action-carry from previous beat. Use rotating transition words (So, Now, Then, Next, Anyway, And). No "Now" twice in a row.
2. Sell the illusion. Name the design intent in plain English BEFORE describing the work. "From across the room, your eye should follow one continuous shape." The why before the what.
3. Action plus reason. Present tense. Every move stated with the why.
4. Jargon-on-contact. Any technical term gets defined inside the same sentence it appears in, or gets swapped for plain language. Examples: "compression bit" + "shears the fibers at both faces so the entry edge stays clean." "SOSS hinge" + "concealed hinge that buries entirely into a two-step mortise."
5. Failure beat (when applicable). Human reaction FIRST, technical explanation SECOND. Groan on mic, then explain. "This is the part where I tell you I should've cut a test piece first. I did not."
6. CRAFT pillar surface (when it lands organically). Pick one of the five pillars and let it land. Do not label it.
7. Open loop or close-of-loop. Either plant ("the joinery starts giving me real problems") or pay off ("remember when I said the stretcher was going to bite me").
8. Transition out. Tell the viewer where they are on the map. "Onto hinges." "Box-to-leg attachment is sorted. Time to build the box."

## CRAFT pillar check (Nick's relatability framework)

For each build step, check which pillars it hits. 3+ pillars = relatable. 0 = process doc, cut or reframe.

- C - Challenge. What stands in the way of completing this step?
- R - Road. Where did Nick start to get to now? (The arc: "third project on the CNC," "two years ago I couldn't open CAD," "I'm one guy in a shop with robots doing the heavy days.")
- A - Art. The why. The design call. The dope thing.
- F - Fire. What aspect lights Nick up? Where's the nerd-out?
- T - Tools. What makes this possible? (CNC, CAD, AI, Shaper Origin, pantorouter, the one-guy-doing-more-than-one-guy-should leverage.)

Surface CRAFT pillars naturally inside the prose. Never label them on screen.

## Word budget math

Use this every time. Keep the math internal; it appears only in supplementary output when Nick asks (see below).

- Read speed cap: 120 words per minute. Slower is fine. Faster is wrong.
- Target window fill: 60-70% of available footage time. 100% is wrong, every time. Build sounds, visuals, and breath need air.
- Math: window seconds * (120/60) * 0.65 = word target. Example: a 51 second window targets ~66 words.
- Put word count and read-seconds on every block before the read. Format: `(~XX words, ~YY sec at 120 wpm)`
- Pre-plan cut points per block: 2-3 trim options, kept internal and delivered only on request.

When trimming for budget: jokes are the LAST thing to cut. Process detail and qualifying clauses go first, never the confessional qualifiers (checklist 20). A joke the joke test in `SKILL.md` (LAYER 4) already cuts was never in the budget; cut it before trimming anything, unless it might be Nick's own, which goes to a response notice.

## Hook / cold open patterns (~30 sec, ~36-42 words VO)

The cold open machine:

- Present tense, viewer-direct, in Nick's voice. Open cold on the hook with no greeting: anti-corpus A7 rejects the warm open ("welcome back to the shop").
- These budgets come from the window math. The window math and the 120 wpm cap are physical and never yield, not even to the exemplar.
- Open the unexpected immediately. Not the setup. The reveal.
- Tease the title number, never explain in the cold open. ("That title? Not clickbait. Stick around.")
- Comedy via visual juxtaposition with VO straight-faced. Let the cuts do the joke while the read stays deadpan.
- Self-aware genre play puts Nick in cahoots with the viewer.
- End on a hard cut to the title card or the design section.
- Sensitive imagery carries the joke that the VO can't say. Keep the read advertiser-safe in the open. The b-roll does what the words can't.

## Body retention engine (sprinkle throughout)

Apply these continuously through the build body. Audit each section against the list.

Continuous loop braiding. Not "one open loop per section." Nick juggles three or four loops at any given moment. Each beat closes one curiosity gap and opens another. The viewer should never have zero unfinished threads in front of them, and never more than they can hold in their head. This is the actual engagement engine, not a checklist item. Track loops live: at any timecode, name what's currently open and what just closed. Aim for 2-4 in flight at all times. Types of loops to keep braided:
- The design tease ("I want this to look like X, but I haven't figured out how yet")
- The deferred problem ("the joinery on the stretcher is going to bite me")
- The result tease ("we'll see if this holds")
- The setup-for-payoff ("you'll see why this matters in a minute")
- The mystery prop ("more on this later")
- The hidden subject ("there's a reason I picked this material, but I'll get to that")

When a loop closes, immediately open another in the same beat or the next one. Never close two in a row without opening one. Never open three in a row without closing one. Keep the texture balanced.

Mind-read the skeptic. "I know a few of you were already wondering." "And because I know what you're thinking." Voice the objection, kill it.

Barrier-lowering. Acknowledge the expensive or specialized tool once upfront, then give the principle so non-tool-owners stay. "A CNC makes this clean, but the idea is just rough it close, then refine with a template and a router." Do not repeat the lowering line every section. Once is enough.

Stakes via irreversibility. Frame design choices as decisions that can't be unmade. "Every fastener has to be reachable after the rest is together. That means deciding right now which face each bolt comes in from, and committing to it."

Tell the viewer where they are on the map. Constant micro-signposting.

Failure-as-trust. Self-deprecating admission of mistakes is the strongest retention tool Nick has. Lean in. Groan first, explain second.

Share-the-excitement. The voice is collaborative, not promotional. When something works, say so with the actual energy of it working ("there it is," "sweet," "look at that," "I'll be damned, that's clean"). When something is cool, share the cool, do not present the cool.

## Sponsor bridge protocol (this is where the drift hurts most)

The lead-in to the sponsor block is a SEAMLESS action-carry transition. Not a topic pivot. Not an "ad break." If it sounds like an ad break, it failed.

Action-carry formula:
1. Identify the active build moment closing the previous beat. (Clamping, drying, curing, walking, opening a door, turning around, finishing a cut.)
2. Use that action as a pun, callback, or physical bridge to the sponsor reveal.
3. Mock-suspense the reveal. ("What could it be... I'll be damned.")
4. Sponsor named as PUNCHLINE, not preamble.

Forbidden lead-in phrasings (these flag the cut as an ad-break and should never appear):
- "This part of the video is powered by"
- "Quick word about"
- "Thanks to our sponsor"
- "Today's video is brought to you by"
- "Speaking of which"
- "Let's take a quick break"

Preferred alternatives:
- Action-carry pun ("one door dries while another opens")
- Self-aware "the reason I can afford to keep the lights on"
- Casual "I'll be damned, it's [BRAND]"

Lead-in runtime cap: 15 seconds. Math at 120 wpm = 30 words max.

Inside the sponsor block:
- The read stays in Nick's voice. Keep the ass jokes, the "no drama," the "I was shocked," the slang.
- Contract-required spec phrasing goes in verbatim. Any meaningful delta between the recorded take and the contract reference is surfaced briefly in the response, outside the deliverable, as Step 4 requires; the detailed contract-delta flags go in the supplementary output when Nick asks. If a delta breaks the contract, ask him before delivering.
- Demo blocks listed in the brief must be visually compliant. If footage may not exist, raise it as a response notice (see Response notices).
- Sponsor owns only the sponsor block. Phallus, drugs, guns, sex jokes, and anything else stays untouched in the rest of the cut. Never PG the video for the sponsor.

Outro callback to sponsor: one short line, ~15-25 words max. Should not feel like the ad is playing twice.

Description requirements (if the sponsor brief specifies them):
- UTM link, discount code, product link, brand hashtags. Track these for the description checklist in the supplementary output, delivered when Nick asks.

## Bookend structure (when a long-form subject deserves it)

If the build includes a wood-species explainer, a material story, or any standalone subject that earns more than 60 seconds of content:

- Split the explainer in half. Front half (hook + accessible curiosity + funny detail) sits in the first third of the video, scored to the first appearance of that material on camera.
- Back half (the heavier toughness/history/stakes/thesis) bookends the video, immediately before the outro reveal.
- The thesis line lives at the end, not buried mid-build, and stays attached to the object rather than standing alone as an aphorism (anti-corpus A1).

Front-half ash-style beats (adapt to any subject): unexpected botanical or historical hook, accessible curiosity detail with a funny note, working-tool spec (Janka or equivalent) framed as why it matters to the build.

Back-half ash-style beats: signature property (toughness, density, figure, whatever), historical/cultural anchor with a memorable image, the stakes/threat/scarcity story, the supply-and-demand or salvage angle, the era thesis.

## Outro patterns (~45 sec without sponsor, +20-25 sec with sponsor callback)

The closer machine:

- Open with a casual "there you have it" or equivalent, in Nick's voice.
- Pay off the title open loop. Use "by my math" for any quantitative claim that can't be perfectly verified.
- Like / Hype ask phrased without begging. "YouTube uses those to shove smaller channels like mine in front of more people. Costs you nothing, helps me a lot."
- Comment ask tied to a real prompt. ("Tell me what you would actually hide in here.") Only include "I read every single one" if Nick actually does.
- Thoughtmarks plug, in voice: "if you can't remember shit, that is the entire reason I built Thoughtmarks. Bookmarks for your brain. App Store or thoughtmarksapp.com." Adapt the qualifier to the build but keep the slangy intro.
- Sponsor callback (one line, light).
- Bookend: return to the cold-open image. `03-youtube-vo.md` makes this a hard rule.
- Sign-off, locked: "I'm Nick, and I'll catch you on the next one. Peace." Use this exact wording, as required by `03-youtube-vo.md`.

Provide alt lines for the spicy vs clean lane on any sensitive button as a response notice, so Nick can pick.

## AI-cadence blacklist (scan before delivery)

Run a find-pass against the final document for these phrases and patterns, under the same rule: a hit BLOCKS the draft; "Rewrite if the phrase is generic; surface it for a decision if it might be his" (`SKILL.md`, LAYER 4, banned phrasebook). Never silently delete phrasing that may be Nick's. Confirmed anti-corpus and no-signaling prohibitions still apply.

Exact phrases:
- "cheaper than it has any right to be"
- "punches above its weight"
- "deceptively simple"
- "more than you'd expect"
- "lives or dies on"
- "hard-won"
- "with that said"
- "by virtue of"
- "the unsung hero"
- "at the end of the day"
- "it's worth noting that"
- "in essence"
- "fundamentally"
- "it goes without saying"
- "speaks volumes"
- "first and foremost"
- "make no mistake"
- "let me be clear"
- "the truth is"
- "navigates the complexity"
- "delve into"
- "showcases"
- "highlights"
- "ensures"
- "leverages" (as a verb in narration)
- "seamlessly" (unless about the sponsor bridge concept)
- "robust"
- "innovative"
- "elevate" / "elevates" / "elevation" (when not literal)

Patterns:
- Aphoristic closers ("X is Y. Z is not." structure). A freestanding aphorism (anti-corpus A1) is always cut. One attached to an object or a failure may be his: surface it for his decision.
- "Not just X, it's Y." pattern (overused).
- Three-beat parallelism for emphasis when the third beat is redundant.
- Symmetric sentence pairs that feel like ad copy.
- "When [profession] [verb]" generalizations.
- Replacement of Nick's confessional qualifiers with confident reframes. ("I think" / "I reckon" / "kind of" / "I guess" / "by my math" / "I'd say" / "pretty sure" / "looks like" / "seems like" all stay.)
- Stage-direction openers ("Pulling X out of Y." / "Removing the clamps." / "Setting up the cut."). These are summary POV. Rewrite from inside the moment with a discourse marker and live discovery if generic; surface it for Nick's decision if it might be his.
- Objective-certainty sentence closes ("X depends on Y." / "The result is Z." / "This determines whether..."). Replace with subjective hedge or live reaction if generic; surface it for Nick's decision if it might be his.
- Sentences that close themselves before the action closes itself. Permit trailing thoughts, dot-dot-dots, and hyphen-pauses.
- Deletion of discourse markers ("Alright," "Well," "So," "Now," "I guess," "Kind of," "Anyway," "Honestly," "Actually," "You know"). These are texture, not filler. If a block has zero, it failed.
- Flattening of reactions into assessments ("Looks good." replacing "That's looking pretty good!"). Exclamation and energy stay when the moment earns them.
- Collapsing recursive looking into single declarations. Doubled observations ("looks like... looking pretty good") are intentional, not redundant.
- Promotional framings ("Here is how I solved it" / "I'm going to show you" / "The result is"). Replace with collaborative framings ("let's see if this works" / "watch this" / "we'll see") if generic; surface it for Nick's decision if it might be his.

Forbidden punctuation:
- Em dashes. Use periods, semicolons, or comma-comma.
- En dashes (except in numeric ranges like 80-120 grit).
- Emojis anywhere.

## Sensitive content rule (explicit)

Phallus stays. Contraband stays. Drug references stay. Sex jokes stay. Profanity stays. Body humor stays. Anatomical specificity stays.

The ONLY exceptions:
1. Inside a sponsor block, if contract requires advertiser-safe phrasing for the sponsor's own section. Even then, the rest of the cut is untouched.
2. If Nick explicitly asks IN THE CURRENT MESSAGE to soften a specific line.

Default action: preserve verbatim. If unsure, ask Nick. Never silently soften.

## Adversarial pre-delivery checklist

Before handing over the assembled script, audit against this list. Fix silently, except where a response notice is required (see Response notices): Nick's own phrasing that a phrasing rule would rewrite or cut and that might be his, or a fix that visibly changes the structure.

1. Em dash, en dash, emoji scan: zero hits, except en dashes in numeric ranges.
2. AI-cadence blacklist, phrases AND patterns: every hit rewritten if generic, or surfaced for Nick's decision as a response notice if it might be his. None left unaddressed.
3. Live discovery POV: every block reads like Nick is finding out right now, not summarizing what already happened. Run the rewrite test on every block. Stage-direction openers ("Pulling X out of Y") rewritten from inside the moment if generic, surfaced as a response notice if they might be his.
4. Discourse markers preserved: at least one per ~75 words of VO (alright, well, so, now, anyway, kind of, sort of, I guess, I mean, you know, honestly, actually, real quick, or at least). Zero markers = failed block; add them back.
5. Reaction over assessment: where the build earns energy, energy is in the read. Where the moment warrants, a generic flat assessment ("Looks good.") is rewritten as a reaction ("That's looking pretty good!"); one that might be Nick's is surfaced as a response notice.
6. Subjective certainty preserved: "I guess," "I think," "I reckon," "by my math," "looks like," "seems like," "pretty sure" all left intact. No replacements with objective-certainty phrasing ("depends on whether," "is determined by," "the result is").
7. Trailing thoughts and hyphen-pauses permitted: dot-dot-dot pauses, mid-sentence pivots, doubled observations are encouraged where they fit Nick's actual speech rhythm.
8. Collaborative framing: blocks read like Nick is at the bench with the viewer, not selling a finished product to them.
9. POV check: every block contains at least one viewer-direct word (you/we/let's/your).
10. Tense check: present tense on on-screen action; past tense only for backstory. AND combined with live discovery POV check.
11. Sentence length: no sentence over ~20 words that isn't broken or breathable, except where the exemplar carries one (The voice lock, Sentence shape).
12. Sensitive content: verbatim preserved.
13. Sponsor lead-in: action-carry, under 15 sec, no forbidden phrasings.
14. Inventory mapped internally: every section of the running order categorized RECORDED or TO-RECORD before any VO was written.
15. Gap labels present: every VO block headed with its timecode range. Word count and read-seconds parenthetical at the end of each block.
16. Continuous loop braiding: 2-4 loops in flight at any given timecode. Track plant-payoff pairs. Never two closes in a row without an open. Never three opens in a row without a close.
17. Jargon: every technical term defined within sentence-of-use or swapped to plain language.
18. CRAFT pillars: one pillar per block at most, surfaced where it lands; across a build step, at least three, or one in every block when the step has fewer than three blocks.
19. Joke density: jokes preserved through trim passes.
20. Confessional qualifiers preserved ("by my math," "I think," "I reckon," "kind of").
21. Throat-clearing self-awareness preserved (the disarmer joke at the start of a nerd-out).

## Output format

Primary deliverable: clean paragraph VO covering ONLY the gaps between on-camera talking-head sections. Read-ready. Each gap labeled with its timecode range. No section titles, no tags, no inventory tables, no editor notes inside the deliverable. Nick's task is to record the gap copy and drop it on A6 between the talking-head clips on A5. The output should look like a clean script Nick can read top-to-bottom into a mic.

Format per gap:

```
[HH:MM:SS - HH:MM:SS]
Paragraph-form VO copy in Nick's voice. Refined from the rambling notes. Present tense, viewer-direct, breath-tested sentences, jargon-on-contact, joke density preserved. Conversational meander preserved. Multiple paragraphs if the gap warrants it.
(~XX words, ~YY sec at 120 wpm)

[HH:MM:SS - HH:MM:SS]
Next gap's VO paragraph. Same rules apply.
(~XX words, ~YY sec at 120 wpm)
```

That is the entire document. Word-count and read-seconds parenthetical at the end of each gap is fine because it's compact and Nick uses it to pace himself. Everything else stays out unless Nick asks.

What does NOT appear in the primary deliverable:
- Inventory tables (RECORDED vs TO-RECORD column lists)
- Tagged blocks ([REC] / [VO] markers)
- Section titles or beat names
- Cut-point trim suggestions
- Open-loop trackers
- CRAFT pillar coverage notes
- AI-cadence scan results
- Sponsor contract-delta flags
- Fact-claim confidence notes
- Description checklists
- Footage gap flags

All of that is supplementary. Build it internally to ensure the read is right. Surface it only if Nick asks for "editor notes," "scan results," "inventory," "budget summary," or similar. Otherwise the document stays clean.

**Response notices.** Separately from the document, the response carries short notices. Two other parts of the response are not notices: the files-read sentence (Operating rules, rule 1) and pre-assembly clarifying questions (When something is missing). Every other rule in this procedure that says flag, surface or ask points to this list or to Supplementary outputs:

- A meaningful sponsor or fact delta (Step 4); a delta that breaks the contract is a question, asked before delivering.
- Nick's own phrasing that a phrasing rule would rewrite or cut and that might be his. The phrasing rules are the voice lock, the rewrite test, the AI-cadence blacklist and the joke test. Words you drafted, and generic phrasing in his inputs that is plainly not his, are rewritten or cut silently. Budget trims and sentence splits of his outline are the haircut (operating rule 4) and are silent too, unless a trim removes a whole line or joke that might be his; that is a notice. A line that might be his stays in the delivered script exactly as he wrote it, and the notice asks whether to change it; if keeping it puts the block over its word budget, the same notice says by how much, and if it would push the read past the 120 wpm cap for the window, the notice says the line does not fit and offers the trim for him to approve.
- A correction that changes his argument (failure mode 3).
- A sensitive-content choice, with the spicy and clean alt lines for him to pick.
- A fix that visibly changes the structure.
- A sponsor demo block whose footage may not exist.
- A section that would normally be REC when no on-camera transcription was provided.
- Any other missing input, named before the script, including where the midroll cut goes when there is no sponsor brief.

## Supplementary outputs (only on request)

If Nick asks for "editor notes," "the scan," "inventory," "budget breakdown," "cut points," "fact flags," "description checklist," or similar, deliver a second document (or a fenced section at the bottom of the same document) with whichever of these the request implies:

- Inventory table (REC vs VO with TC ranges and source file)
- Word budget summary (per gap and total)
- Open-loop tracker (planted -> paid off, with TC of each)
- CRAFT pillar coverage per build step
- AI-cadence scan results (any blacklist hits found, and whether each was rewritten or surfaced)
- Sponsor contract-delta flags (recorded take vs canonical reference)
- Fact-claim confidence notes: high, medium or low for each quantitative or historical claim, with its source
- Cut-point options per gap (2-3 trim suggestions for runtime flexibility)
- Description checklist (UTM, discount code, hashtags, plug links, brand tags)
- Footage gap flags (any REC section where on-camera coverage is uncertain)

Default behavior: do not produce these. Produce them only when explicitly asked.

## Operating rules for assembly

When invoked:

1. Read every source file end-to-end before writing anything. State which files you read in one short sentence at the top of the response (not inside the deliverable).
2. Build the inventory INTERNALLY. It exists to identify the gaps between on-camera sections. Do not surface it in the deliverable.
3. Identify the timecode range of every gap that requires VO. These are the only blocks you write copy for.
4. Use rough draft cadence as voice baseline. Polish goes on top, not over. The refined VO should sound like Nick's rambling notes after a haircut, not after a personality transplant.
5. Run the adversarial checklist internally before delivering.
6. Resolve judgment calls silently when the right answer is clear. Surface only what the Response notices list names.
7. Never re-voice something that's already on camera unless Nick explicitly asks.
8. Never soften sensitive content unless Nick explicitly asks in this message.
9. Never use the forbidden sponsor lead-in phrasings.
10. Never add aphoristic closers of your own. One that might be Nick's follows failure mode 2.
11. Deliver clean paragraph VO with timecode-range labels per gap. Nothing else in the deliverable unless asked.

## When something is missing

If Nick hasn't provided one of the inputs, do not invent it. Name it in a response notice, or ask before assembling where the answer changes the build. Examples:
- No on-camera transcription provided: assemble what you can from the rough outline, list every section that would normally be REC in a response notice, not as a tag in the deliverable.
- No sponsor brief: skip the sponsor block, and give the midroll location in a response notice.
- No timecodes: ask whether to estimate windows from word count, or work without budgets.
- No canonical polished section files: that's fine, on-camera takes are the default anyway.

Ask one question at a time. Do not chain clarifying questions. Get to assembly fast.
