# Sawyer Design, written

Two sub-registers that were collapsed into one and should not be. Nick caught this: the same passage "works as a blog, fails as a portfolio piece."

---

# A. COMMERCIAL (portfolio, services, product copy, anything upstream of the commission page)

**Job:** establish price justification before the reader reaches the commission form. The bespoke craftsman who makes one-off work for wealthy people. Casual and cool but polished. Value carried through story.

**This is his one assertive register.** Hedges run near zero here and that is correct, not an A8 failure. Every other register requires them.

## Rules

- **Imply differentiation, never state it (A15).** "Why this is not a furniture store table" is an authority claim and he never makes those. The specifics are the argument. Never name the competitor, never state the premium.
- **Detailed, not pretentious.** Every claim attaches to a decision, a material, or a number.
- Handmade, tailored to the customer and the space, meticulous attention, premium and exclusive materials. Show these; don't assert them.
- Longevity claims are concrete: "bolted, not glued, so it can be taken apart and moved through a doorway in fifty years."
- **Close on a real CTA pointing at the commission intake form.** Not a trailing observation.
- No emoji. No profanity.
- Burstiness 0.55-0.80 is correct. Written runs flatter than spoken.

## Approved exemplar

> **PROVENANCE: written by Claude, marked SHIP by Nick in blind review.** It is a validated target, not corpus evidence. Do not cite it as proof of how Nick writes. Corpus evidence is material carrying an explicit `HIS` attribution — the canonical index is `devices.md` and `humor-and-hooks.md`, and a register sheet may also carry HIS-marked lines inline (see "His own lines" below). Anything without an explicit HIS marker is not evidence, whatever file it sits in — that is the rule the agency copy and the Claude-written line both got past.
>
> **Oval ash dining table with matched bench.** Commissioned, Portland. Eleven months.
>
> Seats eight. White ash, one tree, so the color will move together for the life of the piece instead of drifting apart. Boards selected in sequence so the grain runs continuously around an ellipse that contains no straight line anywhere. The bench follows the same curve, offset four inches, and from across a room the two read as one object.
>
> The legs are carved, not turned. The edge is a lamé curve rather than a round-over, because a round-over would have left the top looking heavier than the base, and the whole design sends your eye downward. Machined brass hardware. A waterborne finish that will not amber, because this ash should still be pale in thirty years rather than the color of an old gym floor.
>
> There is a concealed compartment. You will not find it. No reveal, no shadow line, no seam that suggests a seam. It opens with a magnetic key hidden in the stretcher, and it took three prototypes to get the fit right.
>
> The base is bolted, not glued, so the table can be taken apart and moved through a doorway in fifty years without damage.
>
> **On the ash.** White ash is critically endangered. The emerald ash borer has killed more than a hundred million trees in North America, and the salvage flood means the wood is briefly available and briefly affordable. In thirty years it will read the way mahogany reads now, as a marker of exactly when a thing was made.
>
> [CTA to commission intake form]

Note the structure: object, then material logic, then design reasoning with the *why* attached, then the hidden thing, then longevity, then provenance, then the ask. No adjective does any work that a specific does not.

# B. ESSAY AND BLOG

**Job:** process and philosophy. This is where his best writing lives and where sincerity is permitted with no undercut.

Longer sentences, burstiness 0.50-0.60, hedges 3-4 per 1k. He is allowed to be earnest here and only here.

**HIS** — his own lines, unused and better than anything generated for him. Corpus evidence under
the provenance rule above; quote and imitate freely:
> "if I stay in the same space too long the plateau becomes the precipice and there is no prize to perfection, only the end of pursuit."
> "The finest heirlooms aren't things, they're memories, stories, and connections."
> "Humans speak curves."
> "Knowledge is a false veneer for the real power of understanding."
> "A designer by fire and a storyteller through wood."

## Approved exemplar

> **PROVENANCE: written by Claude, marked SHIP by Nick in blind review.** It is a validated target, not corpus evidence. Do not cite it as proof of how Nick writes. Only material marked HIS in `devices.md` and `humor-and-hooks.md` is corpus evidence.
>
> Wood does not want to be hollow.
>
> The moment you take material out of one face and not the other, you release tension that has been sitting in those fibers since the tree was standing. Scoop a cavity under a tabletop and the top curls like a potato chip inside a week. It is not a defect. It is the board finally getting to do the thing it has wanted to do since it was cut.
>
> So a hidden compartment in a dining table is not a hiding problem. It is a stress problem, and the answer is that you cannot carve the void. You have to build the mass around it.
>
> The legs became two hollow shells glued around a cavity, which is lighter than solid and has a fraction of the glue surface to clamp. The box between them attaches from the inside out, so nothing reads on the exterior. The rails are faked with a sliding dovetail, so the grain appears to run straight through the leg and out the other side, and the dovetail lets the two pieces move against each other seasonally instead of tearing.
>
> The part I keep thinking about is that almost none of that is visible, and the entire point was that none of it is visible. Eleven months of work whose success condition is that you walk past it.

## Structural note

An essay opens on the thesis, not the project. The build is the evidence. His two highest-performing videos per minute of runtime are thesis-first, and the same ordering works on the page.
